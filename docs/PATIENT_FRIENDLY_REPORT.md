# CareAI Patient-Friendly Report

## Core Principle

CareAI is not a medical database. It is a guide for a non-medical person who has just received a blood test result and wants to understand it in plain language.

The report answers seven questions every patient has:

1. **What test is this?**
2. **Why is this test done?**
3. **What did my report show?**
4. **Which result needs attention?**
5. **What does that result mean in simple words?**
6. **Should I be concerned?**
7. **What can I ask my doctor?**

---

## Report Structure

```
1. Patient information
2. Your report, in simple words  (plain-language summary)
3. What is this test?            (test explanation, CBC only)
4. Results that need attention   (abnormal only, expanded by default)
5. Results within the range      (normal, collapsed by default)
6. Questions for your doctor
7. Please keep in mind           (safety disclaimer)
```

## Patient-Friendly Language Principles

### Use plain comparisons, not labels

| Instead of              | Use                                      |
|-------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| "Out of Range"          | "Higher than the range" / "Lower than the range" |
| "Normal"                | "Within the range"                       |
| "Reference Range"       | "Range shown on your report"             |
| "Elevated WBC"          | "Your white blood cell count is higher…" |
| "Clinical significance" | "What this may mean"                     |
| "Parameter"             | "Result" or "Test"                       |
| "Unable to determine"   | "Could not be read"                      |

### Structure every explanation in four parts

Each abnormal result has four paragraphs, delivered in order:

1. **What is this?** — What the test measures, in simple terms
2. **What did your report show?** — The specific direction of the result (higher/lower)
3. **What does this mean?** — Uncertainty-aware interpretation without a diagnosis
4. **Should I be concerned?** — Calm, honest guidance pointing to the doctor

Normal results have three paragraphs (no "should I be concerned" section).

### Explain abbreviations on first use

Always write: **"White Blood Cells (WBC)"** — never just "WBC" on its own.
Always write: **"Hemoglobin (Hb)"** — never just "Hb" on its own.

### Keep sentences short

- One idea per sentence.
- No sentence longer than 25–30 words where avoidable.
- Use active voice ("Your doctor will explain" not "Explanation will be provided").

### Never diagnose, never falsely reassure

**Wrong:**
- "You have anemia."
- "Don't worry, your results are fine."
- "This proves you have an infection."

**Right:**
- "Your hemoglobin is lower than the range shown on your report."
- "There are several possible reasons for this."
- "Your doctor will look at this together with how you feel."

## Status Labels

| Backend value    | UI label (English)          | Notes                       |
|------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|
| `within_range`   | Within the range            | Green badge with ✓          |
| `above_range`    | Higher than the range       | Amber badge with ⚠           |
| `below_range`    | Lower than the range        | Amber badge with ⚠           |
| `unknown`        | Could not be read           | Neutral badge, no colour    |

Status is communicated with both **color** and **text** (not color alone).

---

## Summary Section

The report opens with a plain-language summary generated by the backend, covering:

- Total number of results checked
- How many are within / outside the range
- Uncertainty-aware framing

Example (English, 1 outside of 3):
> "Out of 3 results checked, 2 are within the range shown on your report and 1 is outside that range. Your doctor will help you understand what this means for you."

Example (all within range):
> "All 3 results checked in this report are within the ranges shown. That is a good sign, though it is still worth discussing your report with your doctor."

The summary is **never** a dashboard of counts ("Normal: 2, Out of Range: 1"). Those numbers appear only as part of a complete sentence.

## Patient Information

Extracted fields:
- **Patient name** — regex match on "Patient Name" or "Name"
- **Age** — regex match on "Age" followed by a number
- **Sex** — regex match on "Sex" or "Gender"
- **Sample date** — regex match on "Sample Date", "Date", "Collected"

If a field is absent from the report, the UI shows: *"Not shown in report"* (in the appropriate language).

**Privacy rules:**
- Patient data is never stored in localStorage.
- Patient data is never in the URL.
- Patient data is never logged to the console in production.
- The only value in localStorage is the language preference (`careai_lang`).

## Expandable Explanation Cards

Each result card shows:

```
[Test name]  [Status badge]              [Value] [Unit]
Range shown on your report: [range]

[Show explanation ▼]
  Paragraph 1: What is this test?
  Paragraph 2: What did your report show?
  Paragraph 3: What does this mean?
  Paragraph 4: Should I be concerned?   (abnormal only)
```

- Abnormal results: **expanded by default**
- Normal results: **collapsed by default** (behind a collapsible section)

---

## Doctor Questions

Questions are short, plain, and practical:

**Good:**
- "Why might my hemoglobin be lower than the range shown?"
- "Do I need a follow-up test?"
- "Is there anything else in my results I should know about?"

**Avoid:**
- "Could you explain what might be causing my hemoglobin to be below the lower limit of the reference interval in relation to my clinical presentation?"

---

## Safety Boundaries

CareAI enforces these safety boundaries at the backend level (`MedicalSafetyValidator`):

1. **Schema validation** — Pydantic ensures all required fields are present.
2. **Keyword scanning** — Regex word-boundary patterns catch unsafe phrases like `\bdiagnose\b`, `\bprescribe\b`, `\bstop taking\b`.
3. **Limitations field** — always present with 4 standard disclaimers.

The limitations field is excluded from the keyword scan because it legitimately references terms like "diagnose" in a safe, negative context ("does not diagnose...").

## Accessibility

- All section headings use proper `h1`/`h2`/`h3` hierarchy.
- Icons are `aria-hidden="true"` — they are decorative only.
- Status badges use `role="status"` and `aria-label`.
- Expandable cards use `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`, `role="region"`, `aria-labelledby`.
- Language selector uses `aria-haspopup`, `aria-expanded`, `role="listbox"`, `role="option"`, `aria-selected`.
- Color is never the only status indicator — text labels always accompany color.
